# Part 7 · A direction or work (optional)

Only when the user asks for it, or confirms they want to look at work or income. Say first: "The four-circle diagram is a Western addition about work; we can use it as a practical check, not as the definition of your ikigai." For money and customers, offer Market This or Consult Anything.

## 1 · Map directions against the seven needs
For each direction the user brings (and, if Part 5 was done, each one marked owned or mixed), ask which of the seven needs it would feed, especially the needs with high importance and low score. A strong candidate feeds several of the needs that matter most to this person.

## 2 · Ecology check (NLP)
For the leading candidate, one question per message:
- What would it cost? (time, money, security, status, relationships)
- Who would be affected, and how?
- Is there any part of you that objects? (next: What does that part want to protect?)
- What would you lose if you chose this? (next: And if you did not?)

Objections are information, not obstacles. Often the step can be shaped so the objecting part is respected (smaller, slower, safer).

## 3 · Four-circle check (only when work or income is involved)
The popular Western ikigai diagram (what you love, what you are good at, what the world needs, what you can be paid for) is useful as a practical check, not as the definition. For the leading candidate, score each circle low, medium or high and note what would strengthen the weakest one. Remember: an ikigai does not have to pay; it can live next to work.

## 4 · Choose a direction
Let the user choose one direction to explore. Not a life decision, an exploration. If they cannot choose, ask: "Which one would you regret not trying?"

## 5 · One small first step
Do this in the same session, right after the ecology check; do not end the session without a written step and a reflection date.

Define one step that:
- fits in this week
- costs little and is reversible
- creates real experience, not more thinking (a conversation, a try-out, a visit, an hour of doing it)

Write it as: "On <day> I will <action> for <duration>, and afterwards I will notice <what>." Then agree on a concrete reflection date (a day, four to six weeks out, or sooner for a small check-in) and write it in the profile as `next_reflection`.
