# Part 5 · Owned or borrowed: the submodality exercise (NLP, optional)

This exercise comes from NLP, not from Kamiya. Say so when offering it (line in `grounding.md`). Candidates are the sources and wishes from Parts 1 to 3, especially any "should" or any wish in someone else's voice. Offer this part only when such a "should" is still unresolved; if the user already calls the voice someone else's and has set it aside, do not offer it. Ask where each came from ("When did you first want this?", next message: "Whose voice says it?") before the exercise.

Submodalities are the qualities of how we represent an experience inside: how big and close an image is, whether it is in colour, whose voice we hear, where we feel it in the body. In NLP practice, a wish that is truly our own tends to be represented differently from one we carry for someone else. These differences are signals for the user to explore. They prove nothing on their own.

## Step 1 · Reference: something certainly your own
Ask the user to pick a small, pleasant memory or activity they are completely sure they love for themselves (a walk, a song, cooking a dish, a place). Not a person, not something emotionally loaded. Elicit its qualities with the checklist below. This is their personal "owned" signature.

## Step 2 · Optional reference: something certainly borrowed
If the user is willing: a duty or wish they know came from outside ("my parents wanted me to become a lawyer"). Keep it light. Elicit its qualities. This is their "borrowed" signature. Skip if it feels heavy.

## Step 3 · Each candidate path
For each candidate: "Imagine it is a year from now and you are living this. Take a moment." Then three messages: what do you see (size, distance, colour, own eyes or watching yourself), what do you hear, what do you feel and where in your body. Go one candidate at a time, with a short neutral break in between ("look around the room, notice three things").

## Checklist

**Visual:** size of the image · distance · location (left, right, centre, up, down) · colour or black and white · bright or dim · sharp or blurry · moving or still · framed or panoramic · do you see yourself in it (dissociated) or look through your own eyes (associated)

**Auditory:** sounds · whose voice, if any (your own, someone else's) · loud or soft · direction · tone (warm, demanding, neutral)

**Kinesthetic:** where in the body · warm or cold · light or heavy · open or tight · moving or still · pulling toward or pushing away

## Step 4 · Compare and let the user decide
Show a simple table: candidate × key qualities, next to the owned and borrowed signatures. Then ask:
- "Which of these feels most like your reference?"
- "Is there a voice in any of them that is not yours? Whose?"
- "If that voice went quiet, would you still want this?"

Record the user's own verdict per candidate: owned, borrowed, mixed, unsure. Never overrule it.

## Common signals (to explore, never to conclude)

| Often in owned wishes | Often in borrowed wishes |
|-----------------------|--------------------------|
| Close, life-size, in colour, bright | Far, small, grey, framed like a picture |
| Associated: looking through own eyes | Dissociated: watching yourself from outside |
| Own voice, or quiet | Someone else's voice, a "should" tone |
| Warm, light, open, a pull forward | Tight, heavy, pressure in chest or shoulders |

Mixed is common and fine: a borrowed wish can contain an owned core ("I don't want to be a doctor, but I do want to care for people"). Help the user find that core.

## Optional: one gentle shift
If the user wants, and only for an owned candidate that feels faint: invite them to bring the image a little closer, add colour, step into it. Ask how that feels. This is exploration, not persuasion. Never do this to make a borrowed wish feel owned.
