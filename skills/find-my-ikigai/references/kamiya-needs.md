# Kamiya's seven ikigai needs

Japanese psychiatrist Mieko Kamiya (1914 to 1979) studied what made life feel worth living for people in the hardest circumstances, among them the patients of the Aisei-en leprosarium on Nagashima. In *Ikigai ni tsuite* (1966) she described seven needs behind ikigai-kan, the feeling that life is worth living. Not everyone needs all seven; each person has their own combination.

For each need, in one message: a one-line explanation, then "How fulfilled is this need now, from 0 to 10, and what moment makes you say that?" (this pair may stay one question). If the user gives no score, ask only for the score next. After all seven, ask once: "Which three matter most to you?" Mark those `high`; record the others as `not in top three`, never as a level the user did not give.

Grounding: use the Part 4 lines in `grounding.md` (life satisfaction and a bright future have their own lines).

| # | Need | What it is about | Questions |
|---|------|------------------|-----------|
| 1 | Life satisfaction | Feeling that life is on the right track, contentment with what is | When did you last think "this is good"? What makes an ordinary day satisfying? |
| 2 | Change and growth | Learning, developing, not standing still | What are you learning right now that excites you? Where do you feel stuck? |
| 3 | A bright future | Hope, something to look forward to, goals and dreams | What are you looking forward to? What would you love to exist in five years? |
| 4 | Resonance | Connection, being understood, sharing, mattering to others | With whom do you feel truly met? Who needs you, and do you like being needed that way? |
| 5 | Freedom | Choosing for yourself, autonomy over time and direction | Where in your life do you choose freely? Where do you live by someone else's choice? |
| 6 | Self-actualization | Expressing who you are, using your gifts | When do you feel you are fully using what you have? What part of you is not expressed? |
| 7 | Meaning and value | Feeling that what you do matters, contributes, has worth | What would you want to have contributed? What feels meaningful even if nobody sees it? |

## Patterns to explore (ask first)

- **High importance, low score:** the strongest pull for change.
- **High score, low importance:** something that works but may not be theirs to keep chasing.
- **One need carrying all the weight:** fragile ikigai; explore other sources.
- **Needs met only through one role or person:** explore what happens if that changes.

Record scores and importance in the profile. They feed the map.

## Linking needs to sources (after the scores)

Show the scores next to the sources from Part 3 and ask, one need at a time, starting with the important and low ones: "Which of your sources feeds this need?" When you link something the user said to a need, quote their words and let them name the need; do not file it yourself. A need without any source is a gap; a need fed by one fragile source is at risk. Record `needs[].fed_by`.
