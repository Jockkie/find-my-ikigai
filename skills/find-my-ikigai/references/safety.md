# Safety

Questions about meaning can touch deep places. This skill is reflective coaching. It is not therapy, diagnosis or crisis support.

## State check at the start

Ask one question: "How are you doing today, on a scale from 0 to 10?" Never combine it with a content question. In the next message, if relevant, ask whether now is a good moment for a deeper conversation. If the score is low or the moment is not right, offer a lighter version (only parts 1 and 2) or another moment.

## Stop the exercise when you notice

- talk of not wanting to live, self-harm, or feeling that life has no worth at all
- signs of severe depression, panic, dissociation or psychosis
- trauma surfacing (abuse, violence, loss) with strong distress
- the user saying they are in therapy and this touches that work, unless they want to continue lightly

## What to do

1. Stop the exercise. Respond with warmth, without alarm. Acknowledge what they shared.
2. Ask how they are right now and whether they are safe. It is right to ask directly and calmly: "Are you having thoughts of ending your life?" Asking does not put the idea in someone's head; it opens the door.
3. If there is any risk to life: encourage contact with a crisis line or emergency services right away, and with someone they trust.
   - Netherlands: 113 Zelfmoordpreventie (113 or free 0800-0113, also chat at 113.nl). Immediate danger: 112.
   - Belgium (Flanders): Zelfmoordlijn 1813. Immediate danger: 112.
   - Elsewhere: help them find the local crisis line; in the EU, 112 reaches emergency services.
4. For ongoing distress: suggest a GP, psychologist or therapist.
5. Do not continue the ikigai exercise in that session. Offer to come back to it when they feel steadier.

## Submodality work

Imagining futures is usually gentle, but it can bring up grief or fear. Always let the user choose the reference memory themselves, keep it positive and neutral, and let them stop at any moment. Never push a person to "go into" a painful image.
